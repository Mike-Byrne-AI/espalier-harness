| `DEF-1113` | tools/cc/hooks/_explain_path.py::_net_verdict; espalier/assets/claude/commands/status.md (--explain); tools/cc/session_resume.py | **`/status --explain <path>` ends with "writes allowed (unprotected)" for any path Espalier's own zones do not cover, with no qualifier, although a PreToolUse hook wired beside Espalier's or a `permissions.deny` Edit/Write rule may deny the write.** `tools/cc/hooks/_explain_path.py` builds its verdict only from Espalier's predicates (`_protected_zones._is_protected` and `_is_allowed`, `adopter_zone_for`, `plan_guard._is_exempt`) and never reads `.claude/settings.json`, `settings.local.json` or user settings. The else-branch of `_net_verdict` emits the bare "writes allowed (unprotected)" whenever Espalier's zones do not match. The docstring lists the runtime factors left out (kill-switch, dangerous Bash, MCP, hardlink inode) but not these two, and `status.md` says the read-out "cannot drift from enforcement". A `permissions.deny` path rule is the clean case: every Edit and Write is denied while the read-out says allowed; a co-wired content-checking hook is only partly contradicted. Mitigations keep it small: the per-hook lines are labelled `plan_guard` and `write_guard`, the `--help` text scopes the command to those two, and the real deny still fires with its own message. The cost is a confident wrong answer that a person or agent repeats ("this path is unprotected").
